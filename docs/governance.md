# Governance

Governance has two layers: a core that is not put to a vote, and practical decisions made by the members.

## The core

The core is written into the statutes of the association and can be changed only by a qualified majority of the council of keepers, not by a general vote.

- The dignity of every person. Nobody is humiliated, ranked or sold.
- Truth. No dark patterns, inflated numbers, hidden advertising or fake reviews.
- Service instead of retention. No endless feed and no engagement algorithms.
- Protection of children and the family. No sexual content, gambling or trade in the attention of minors.
- Mercy in moderation. Rules known in advance, decisions explained, an appeal and a way back.
- Open to all. Anyone who accepts these rules may take part, believer or not. Faith is neither checked nor imposed.
- The basics are free forever: a space, questions and answers, the map, a voice.
- A ceiling on the fee taken from a sale.

The network is not a theological authority and belongs to no denomination. The council of keepers is drawn from people of different traditions. It can stop a decision that breaks the core. It does not vote on money or features.

## Voice

- Every confirmed space has one voice.
- There is no weight formula. The network gives nobody a larger voice for activity, seniority or contribution.
- A voice cannot be bought, sold or obtained through a paid plan.
- Votes cover practical matters: what to build next, prices, use of surplus, rules of a place or community.

## Leaders and the delegated voice

People make someone a leader; the network does not. A leader's weight is the number of voices entrusted to them.

- A voice can be entrusted in full or for one area: the affairs of a place, of a community, of a lab. Different people can be chosen for different areas.
- Trust is withdrawn at any moment and takes effect at once. If a person votes themselves, their own vote counts in that decision.
- Trust lasts a year and must then be confirmed.
- A leader cannot pass entrusted voices on.
- A leader votes in the open. An ordinary member votes in secret.
- Whom a person entrusted is visible only to that person, unless they choose to publish it.
- A leader's mandate, how many people in which area, is shown where decisions are made. There are no lists comparing leaders.
- One leader cannot hold more than a set share of the voices in one decision (proposed: 10%). The excess is not counted and the people concerned are told.
- Asking for voices in exchange for money, gifts or discounts is forbidden and forfeits the entrusted voices.
- The core cannot be changed by the voices of leaders.

A community chooses its lead the same way.

## Development of communities and places

Spiral Dynamics is used openly as the skeleton of development for communities and places. It is never applied to individual people ([ADR 0003](adr/0003-no-individual-scoring.md)).

| Stage         | Colour in the model | What the community has built                                | What the network offers at this stage          |
| ------------- | ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Footing    | Beige               | Nobody is left alone in need: food, shelter, help when ill  | Requests for help, a mutual aid fund           |
| 2. Our own    | Purple              | Shared gatherings, traditions, feasts, memory of the place  | Events, calendar, history of the place         |
| 3. Initiative | Red                 | People who take things on; first things carried through     | Projects, quick fundraisers                    |
| 4. Order      | Blue                | Rules, roles, accounts, a way to settle disputes            | Community charter, roles, voting, ledger       |
| 5. Trade      | Orange              | The community earns and teaches: shop, masters, apprentices | Shared shopfront, labs, revenue sharing        |
| 6. Care       | Green               | The weak and the new are heard; decisions by consent        | Consent formats, mentoring of newcomers        |
| 7. Links      | Yellow              | Works with other communities and helps neighbours           | Alliances of communities, cross-place projects |
| 8. The whole  | Turquoise           | Serves the place and the whole network, not only itself     | Network-wide projects, the council of keepers  |

How to read it:

- It is not a ladder to climb and leave behind. A community has a profile across all eight stages, each marked built, weak or absent.
- A community strong in Trade with an empty Footing is not ahead of others. It has a hole in its foundation, and the network suggests starting there.
- The profile of a place is made of the profiles of its communities and shows what the place lacks. Places are not ranked against each other.

How a stage is determined:

- From facts in the network: were requests for help answered, do gatherings happen, were projects finished, is there a charter and an account, are there shared efforts with other communities.
- The list of criteria is open and the same for everyone. No neural network takes part.
- A community sees why a stage is marked as it is and can contest it.

Limits of the model:

- Spiral Dynamics is a working scheme without rigorous scientific validation. The methodology says so, and the stages are tested on the first real communities and revised.
- A stage describes how a community is arranged, not the worth of its people. The model is used as a method, not as a teaching.
- In the interface the stages have plain names. The model's name and colours appear in the open methodology.

## Legal form

A non-profit association under French law (loi 1901). No shares and no owners; it cannot be sold or listed, and on dissolution its assets pass to another non-profit. The founder is technical lead, not owner.
